Every report, free to download

The short version: anyone can download Sidewalk's public reports to study, map, or bring to a council meeting. It's the same information anyone can already see on the map. It never includes names, emails, accounts, or photos.

What's in it

Fields in the download
ColumnWhat it means
idA unique ID for the report.
title, descriptionWhat the resident wrote.
categoryStreet safety, Accessibility, Street lighting, Parks & public space, or Neighborhood issue.
statusSubmitted, Reviewed for clarity, Neighborhood priority, Sent to the city, In progress, or Fixed.
areaThe city or area chosen when it was posted, like "Charlotte, NC".
latitude, longitudeWhere it was pinned (about 1 m precision).
reported_at, status_updated_at, fixed_atDates and times in UTC (ISO 8601). fixed_at is blank until it's fixed.
days_to_fixDays from report to fix, for fixed reports.
backersHow many verified neighbors back it.
photosHow many photos it has (the photos themselves are on the report page).
urlA link to the report on Sidewalk.

Ways to use it

  • Spreadsheets: open the CSV in Excel, Numbers, or Google Sheets. For a sheet that stays up to date, put this in a Google Sheets cell: =IMPORTDATA("/api/open/reports.csv")
  • Maps: drop the GeoJSON file into a free tool like geojson.io, QGIS, or ArcGIS.
  • Apps and class projects: both links work directly from code, from any website.

Reusing it

You're free to use, share, and build on this data. Please credit "Sidewalk community reports" and link back here. Keep in mind:

  • Reports are written by residents. They aren't official government records, and Sidewalk doesn't inspect them on site.
  • Backing counts and statuses change over time, so note the date you downloaded.
  • Don't use the data to find, contact, or target the people who posted it.
