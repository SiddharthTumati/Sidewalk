Community Guidelines

The short version: report real problems in public places, be specific and respectful, protect people's privacy, and keep endorsements honest.

Sidewalk works when neighbors can trust what they see on the map. These guidelines apply to everything you post. Breaking them can get a report removed or an account suspended.

Report real, public problems

  • Report things in public spaces: streets, sidewalks, crosswalks, streetlights, curb ramps, parks, and public signs.
  • Be specific. Say where it is ("north side of Pine St, between 6th and 8th") and what's wrong.
  • One problem per report.
  • Check the map first. If someone already reported it, back their report instead of posting a new one. Sidewalk will suggest this if it spots a likely duplicate nearby.
  • Don't post jokes, tests, or problems you haven't seen yourself.

Be respectful

  • No insults, threats, harassment, hate speech, or swearing.
  • Describe the problem, not a person. Don't blame or name private individuals ("my neighbor at 12 Elm never mows").
  • No ads, links, or self-promotion, and no campaigning for candidates or causes.

Photo rules

  • Take photos only from public places, and only where it's safe. Never step into traffic or trespass for a picture.
  • No faces or license plates in focus. If someone walks into the shot, take another one.
  • No photos inside homes, cars, or private property, and none that show children.
  • Only post photos you took yourself or have permission to use.

Protect privacy

  • Don't post names, phone numbers, or emails of private people.
  • The street address or intersection of the problem is fine. Someone's personal home address isn't.

Keep endorsements honest

  • One person, one account, one endorsement.
  • Don't create extra accounts, use bots, or ask people who don't live nearby to inflate a count.
  • New accounts' endorsements start counting after a day, and bursts of new accounts from one network are reviewed by a coordinator.

Safety first

Emergencies go to 911, not Sidewalk. That includes downed power lines, gas smells, flooding roads, and anything that puts someone in danger right now.

If you see something wrong

Open the report in the Tracker and tap Flag this report. When several neighbors flag a report, it's hidden until a coordinator reviews it. Coordinators can restore reports, remove them, or suspend accounts that break these guidelines.
